Do LLMs have goals?

We investigate whether and how LLMs encode goals: internal representations that precede and guide action, and that are flexible enough to be realized through many different execution paths. In a coding setting with multiple sub-tasks, we localize goal-seeking causal effects in the output of a sparse set of attention heads. The same heads let us decode the model's upcoming action in natural language, e.g. “I am counting unique values” or “I'm checking if a word is a palindrome.”

This encoding is causal for the model's next action, plastic (transplanted into another programming language, it triggers the same operation with different tokens), and sustained while the action is carried out. We present a theory of how a depth-bounded transformer can keep such a goal across long generations by frequently re-deriving it, and find that these goal heads also generalize beyond code, to natural text and few-shot in-context learning.

GoalScope: reading out the upcoming sub-goal

GoalScope verbalizes what the goal heads encode at a single token, several tokens before the model starts writing the corresponding code. The demo shows read-outs on HumanEval, MBPP and classic algorithms for Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct.
